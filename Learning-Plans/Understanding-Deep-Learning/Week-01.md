# Week 01 — Introduction

**Source:** *Understanding Deep Learning*, Chapter 1  
**Schedule:** 3 sessions, choose your own days  
**Mode:** independent English reading/video first → explanation/practice → ChatGPT assessment  
**Expected time:** about 40–60 minutes per session; flexible  
**Textbook:** https://udlbook.github.io/udlbook/

## Session 1 — Supervised Learning

**Read:** Chapter 1, §1.1 *Supervised Learning* (stop before §1.2).  
**Goal:** Understand what is learned from labeled examples and distinguish regression from classification.

- [ ] Read §1.1 independently in English; study the figures and examples.
- [ ] Identify the input, target, and prediction in at least one supervised-learning problem.
- [ ] Explain regression vs. classification, with an original example of each.
- [ ] Practice: for house area → price, identify inputs and labels; explain what happens if training prices are removed.
- [ ] Answer the three assessment prompts below **without looking at model answers**.
- [ ] Discuss your answers with ChatGPT and address key misunderstandings.
- [ ] Session 1 assessment passed (check only after feedback).

**After this session, I can:**
- Explain supervised learning in my own English words.
- Identify inputs, labels, and predictions.
- Classify a new example as regression or classification and explain why.

**Assessment prompts:**
1. In your own words, what is supervised learning? What role do labeled examples play?
2. A model predicts tomorrow's website visitor count. Another predicts whether a user will click an advertisement. Which task is regression and which is classification? Explain.
3. You want to recognize plant species from photographs. Describe the inputs, labels, training examples, and a way to test generalization on new photos.

## Session 2 — Learning Paradigms

**Read:** Chapter 1, §1.2 *Unsupervised Learning* and §1.3 *Reinforcement Learning*.  
**Goal:** Compare supervised, unsupervised, and reinforcement learning, including their feedback signals.

- [ ] Read §1.2–§1.3 independently; optionally watch an English explainer after reading.
- [ ] Make a small English comparison table: data, objective, feedback, and example for each paradigm.
- [ ] Explain agent, environment, action, and reward in one example.
- [ ] Analyze which learning paradigms might be involved in a recommender or image-generation system.
- [ ] Explain your comparison to ChatGPT and complete follow-up questions.
- [ ] Session 2 assessment passed (check only after feedback).

**After this session, I can:**
- Compare three major learning paradigms without merely memorizing definitions.
- Explain why real applications may combine approaches.

**Self-check prompts:**
1. What is the difference between having labeled targets and discovering structure in data?
2. How does a reward signal differ from a supervised label?
3. Can one AI application use multiple learning paradigms? Give a reasoned example.

## Session 3 — Context, Ethics, and Synthesis

**Read:** Chapter 1, §1.4–§1.7.  
**Goal:** Understand the chapter's broader context and use the ideas to evaluate an AI application.

- [ ] Read §1.4–§1.7 independently; identify one unresolved question.
- [ ] Choose one AI application, such as organizing handwritten notes.
- [ ] Describe its input, output, possible learning approach(es), and at least one risk.
- [ ] Write an **80–120-word English summary** of what you learned this week.
- [ ] Complete a weekly discussion and comprehensive assessment with ChatGPT.
- [ ] Session 3 / weekly assessment passed (check only after feedback).

**After this session, I can:**
- Discuss an AI application beyond a simple definition.
- Describe at least one limitation, reliability concern, or ethical risk.
- Explain the main ideas of Chapter 1 in clear English.

**Weekly assessment prompts:**
1. Explain the difference between supervised, unsupervised, and reinforcement learning with examples.
2. Why might a highly accurate model still be unreliable or harmful in practice?
3. Design an AI tool for organizing handwritten notes. What would be learned, from what data, and how might you evaluate it?
