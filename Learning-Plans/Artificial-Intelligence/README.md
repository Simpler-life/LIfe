# Artificial Intelligence Foundations — Classical AI + ZJU Introduction

**Goal:** understand AI beyond neural networks: reasoning, search, adversarial games, probabilistic models, planning, learning and limits. This is a FULL independent track, not just MCTS exam preparation.

## Exact sources
- [Berkeley CS188 free textbook](https://inst.eecs.berkeley.edu/~cs188/textbook/) — use chapter nav (1 Search, 2 CSP, 3 Games including §3.5 MCTS, 4 MDP, 5 RL, 6 Bayes Nets, 8 HMMs, 9 ML, 10 Logic).
- [CS188 official course](https://inst.eecs.berkeley.edu/~cs188/) — lecture/project resources when publicly accessible.
- **ZJU textbook and PPTs (already supplied in the ChatGPT project)**: 《人工智能引论》（吴飞、潘云鹤） Ch 2–10, especially Ch 2 Knowledge Representation, Ch 3 Search, Ch 4 Machine Learning. Consult local materials for course-specific nomenclature and topics not covered in CS188.
- [StatQuest video index](https://statquest.org/video-index/) — regression, classification, trees, PCA, K-means.
- [3Blue1Brown neural networks](https://www.3blue1brown.com/topics/neural-networks) — visual intuition to pair with UDL.

## Learning protocol
1. Read one **exact CS188 subsection** and actively work the figures, not just watch.
2. Define state/actions/objective, assumptions, and failure cases in own words.
3. Solve a **fresh** hand-trace or mathematical example **before** looking up an explanation.
4. When CS61A skills permit, implement one smallest relevant algorithm, test edges.
5. Explain what classical method does **better/differently** than learned neural models.
6. Take R/P/T/F checkpoint: [rubric](../../Progress/Checkpoint-Rubric.md). Do not skip because slides can be memorized.
